Local vs. global robustness

Zhang et al. 2022 formalize the distinction (after Katz et al.): local robustness holds if no perturbation within a bound around a given input changes the output; global robustness holds if this is true for (almost) all inputs. Most testing techniques, including those below, are local — they search for a specific counterexample near a specific input. (source: Zhang et al. 2022)

AORTA / Adaptive Beam Search (Xiao et al. 2025)

First automated black-box robustness-testing framework for the combined prompt+example input space of LLM-based NLP software (prior work perturbed prompts and examples separately). Frames testing as combinatorial optimization over goal function / perturbation / constraint / search method.

Its search method, Adaptive Beam Search (ABS), improves on classic word-importance-ranked beam search with:

  • Adaptive beam width — recomputed each iteration from how many candidates reduced confidence in the ground-truth label.
  • Backtracking — retains a historical-best candidate so early greedy mistakes don’t permanently lose good perturbations.

Result: 86.1% average attack success rate vs. 68.2% for the best baseline (PWWS), with far fewer queries and more grammatically natural adversarial examples. (source: Xiao et al. 2025). See AORTA for full details.

ABFS / Best-First Search (Xiao et al. 2026)

Same group, same joint prompt+example framing and goal/perturbation/constraint/search decomposition as AORTA, with the search method changed:

  • Best-First Search over a full priority queue keyed on the threat model’s confidence in the ground-truth label — expands the globally best node in the whole queue each step, not the beam-local best, escaping local optima that greedy search stalls in.
  • Adaptive Word Importance Ranking — each word’s importance score is adjusted by the average of its historical score changes over the last k2 = 5 perturbations, so fewer words need perturbing.
  • Transformation space is WordNet synonyms filtered by cosine similarity (top k1 = 25); constraints are stop-word filtering and a maximum change rate.

Result: average success rate 74.425% vs 68.177% for the best baseline (PWWS), average change rate 1.009%, average perplexity 8.161 below the best baseline, and ~585 s faster per successful test case. (source: Xiao et al. 2026). See ABFS for full details.

Relation to metamorphic testing

MMT4NL’s “Robustness” and “Fairness” MR categories (Racharak et al. 2026) are effectively lightweight, manually-constructed adversarial perturbations (typos, gender/race swaps) evaluated as pass/fail against a metamorphic relation rather than an optimization objective. Adversarial search (AORTA) and metamorphic testing are two ends of the same spectrum: MT checks a specific, human-defined invariance; adversarial search hunts for any input-space direction that breaks the model. Safety-refusal robustness: consistency testing vs. attack optimization

LEST (Moshood et al. 2026) perturbs safety-critical prompts too, but with a different objective from AORTA/ABFS and from jailbreak fuzzers (PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN, FuzzLLM, and others it cites). Its mutations are generated independently of the target model’s responses — no loop optimizes toward compliance — and the question is whether documented refusal behavior stays invariant under meaning-preserving edits (lexical substitution, modifier insertion/deletion, person conversion, paraphrase, identity swap). The perturbation constraints echo the ones above (grammaticality, perplexity within 2× of the seed, embedding similarity ≥0.80 then ≥0.85) but the success test differs: a candidate counts as a failure only after humans confirm it still asks for the same harmful thing and the response genuinely complies. Results: both models refuse over 95% of unmodified seeds; of 312 automatically flagged Refuse→Comply flips, 19 (6.1%) were validated, spread over five of seven operators. (source: Moshood et al. 2026)
