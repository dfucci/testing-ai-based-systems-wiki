Why ML testing differs from traditional testing

Behavior is data-driven, not manually coded; the system evolves as new data arrives; behavior is emergent and hard to decompose into testable units; and the oracle problem is acute — there is often no pre-existing correct answer. Table 1 of the paper contrasts ML vs. traditional testing across 7 dimensions (component under test, behavior stability, test input, test oracle, adequacy criteria, false positive rate, who plays “tester”). (source: Zhang et al. 2022)

The four-axis taxonomy

(source for this section: Zhang et al. 2022)

  1. Testing workflow (how to test): input generation, oracle identification, adequacy evaluation, prioritization/reduction, bug-report analysis, debug/repair, frameworks/tools.
  2. Testing properties (what to test): functional — correctness, model relevance (over/underfitting); non-functional — robustness/security, efficiency, fairness, interpretability, privacy.
  3. Testing components (where the bug is): data, learning program, framework/library.
  4. Application scenarios: autonomous driving, machine translation, natural language inference.

Key findings

(source for all counts and percentages in this section: Zhang et al. 2022)

  • Strong imbalance toward supervised learning (119/144 papers) vs. unsupervised (3) and reinforcement learning (1) — despite comparable general research popularity of the latter two. METTLE is a rare unsupervised-learning testing paper filling exactly this gap.
  • 93/144 papers focus on correctness/robustness; interpretability, privacy, efficiency, model relevance each have fewer than 6 papers.
  • 38.9% of papers target deep learning specifically.
  • 38.2% published in SE venues (ICSE/FSE/ASE/ICST/ISSTA) vs. only 19.4% in AI venues — ML testing is predominantly an SE research topic.
  • Tool support is immature: no equivalent of Defects4J (a standard ML-bug benchmark) exists.

Fairness taxonomy (notable formal apparatus)

(source for this section: Zhang et al. 2022)

Fairness through unawareness; group fairness (demographic parity, equalized odds, equal opportunity); counterfactual fairness; individual fairness. Barocas & Selbst’s five causes of unfairness: skewed sample, tainted examples, limited features, sample size disparity, proxies.

Limitation

Pre-dates the LLM era entirely — no coverage of prompt-based systems, RAG, or foundation-model-specific evaluation. Every LLM-testing paper in this wiki (MMT4NL, LLMorph, AORTA, REAG, Tangent) extends this taxonomy into territory Zhang et al. couldn’t have surveyed.
