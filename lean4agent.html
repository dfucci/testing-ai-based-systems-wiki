Summary: Lean4Agent uses Lean4 (a dependent-type formal language) to statically verify LLM agent workflow specifications before execution and to localize execution-time failures within trajectories afterward; verified workflows outperform failing ones by 11.94% on average, and its LeanEvolve refinement loop improves SWE-Bench performance by a further 7.47%.

Sources: Wang et al. - 2026 - Lean4Agent Formal Modeling and Verification for Agent Workflow and Trajectory.pdf

Last updated: 2026-10-18

Motivation

Existing agent-verification work is fragmented: LLM-as-judge evaluates outputs but is vulnerable to hallucination/overconfidence; Hoare-style contracts check tool calls; SMT-backed verification checks action-level policies; temporal-logic checkers verify artifacts. Each addresses only part of the problem and is limited by its formal language’s expressiveness (temporal logic can’t model data-dependent properties; SMT contracts struggle with higher-order reasoning). No prior framework formally models and verifies both agent workflow specification and execution trajectory in one unified system. (source: Wang et al. 2026)

Approach

FormalAgentLib is a three-layer extensible Lean4 library:

  • Layer 1 (structural verification): type-checks the workflow as a graph of typed nodes/edges (analogous to compiler-level checks) — catches read/write inconsistencies, e.g. a step reading a variable no reachable predecessor produced.
  • Layer 2 (static semantic verification): models each workflow step as a Hoare-style pre-/post-condition pair over a predicate system, and verifies — under an explicit LLMExec assumption (the LLM correctly executes an instruction given its precondition) — that every step’s preconditions are entailed by previously established postconditions. This is proof-based verification of workflow design, before any execution happens.
  • Layer 3 (execution trajectory verification): checks whether the LLMExec assumption actually held on a specific run, using Lean-checkable predicates, external validators (e.g. Python for URL checks), and LLM-as-judge for predicates only expressible in natural language. It localizes which step in the trajectory is responsible for a failure.

LeanEvolve, built on top, uses Layer-3 failure localization (plus optional environment feedback such as test error messages) to have an LLM revise the specific violating part of a workflow — “formal-guided evolve” — complemented by a broader “pure-LLM evolve” mode for unconstrained exploration. (source: Wang et al. 2026)

Findings

Evaluated on a hard 50-problem subset of SWE-Bench-Verified and 100 problems from ELAIP-Bench (AI paper understanding) across 5 LLMs: workflows that pass Layer-2 static verification outperform failing ones by 14.80% on SWE-Bench and 9.07% on ELAIP-Bench on average (statistically significant, 95% CI excludes zero in nearly all model/task pairs). LeanEvolve’s formal-guided refinement further improves SWE-Bench accuracy by 7.47% on average over the already-verified baseline, and beats pure-LLM evolution by 7.00 percentage points on ELAIP-Bench — predicate-based diagnostics locate the actual defect more precisely than an LLM re-reading its own trajectory. An ablation shows graph-level predicates (not just per-node local predicates) are responsible for most of Layer-2’s detection power: removing them flips 21/40 previously-failing workflows to passing verification, i.e. most real defects are workflow-wide consistency violations that a purely local predicate can’t see. (source: Wang et al. 2026)

Relation to broader wiki

Lean4Agent sits at a different point in the agent-testing lifecycle than AgentSpec’s runtime enforcement: AgentSpec intercepts and constrains actions during execution, while Lean4Agent’s Layer 1/2 verify the workflow specification before any execution happens (design-time, correct-by-construction), and its Layer 3 diagnoses failures after execution by localizing which step broke the LLMExec assumption. It is a formal-methods counterpart to LLM-as-judge evaluation — the paper explicitly compares its Layer-2 verification against a pure LLM-as-judge baseline and finds the LLM judge captures only explicit workflow behavior, missing implicit information flows and graph-level constraints that Lean4Agent’s predicate system catches directly.
