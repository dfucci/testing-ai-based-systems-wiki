Summary: Adds failure diversity as an explicit search objective to STELLAR, a genetic-algorithm test generator for LLM-based applications, via an archive of past failures and a repopulation operator — the greedy-sampling variant (STELLAR-DS) matches random sampling’s failure diversity while retaining much higher failure-detection effectiveness.

Sources:

  • Sorokin et al. - 2026 - Diversity-Guided Search-Based Testing of Large Lan.pdf (arXiv:2609.23209v1, 19 Sep 2026)

Last updated: 2026-10-18

See Search-Based Testing for LLM Applications for the full technical writeup of the method (feature encoding, genetic operators, archive/repopulation mechanism, NSGA-II) and the headline effectiveness/diversity trade-off results.

Evaluation setup

Three case studies, five systems under test, eight LLMs, 18 configurations, >1M executed tests, ~48 GPU-days total:

  • SafeQA: does a standalone LLM produce unsafe responses to malicious prompts (SORRY-bench categories, BeaverTails-derived judge)? Six LLMs tested (GPT-4o, GPT-5-Chat, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Qwen3-8B, Gemma4-E4B, GPT-OSS-120B).
  • NaviQA-I/II: RAG-based in-car venue-recommendation assistant — I is open-source (static Yelp dataset), II is BMW’s industrial prototype (dynamic API retrieval + vehicle-state/manual access).
  • CarQA-I/II: LLM-driven car-function control (windows, lights, climate, seat heating) — I open-source, II an industrial BMW prototype.

Source inconsistency: the paper’s text lists GPT-5-Chat among the six SafeQA models, but its Table 5 SafeQA rows show DeepSeek-V2-16B (and no GPT-5-Chat row); the DeepSeek-V2-16B figures quoted under RQ1 below come from Table 5. Which model was actually run is unresolved in the source.

Baselines: Random Search (RS), T-wise combinatorial (4-wise), ASTRAL (SafeQA only — its coverage-matrix approach doesn’t extend to the larger NaviQA/CarQA feature spaces).

Results

RQ0 — judge evaluation

GPT-4o-mini gave the best accuracy/cost/latency balance as the LLM-judge oracle across all three domains (F1 0.79 SafeQA binary, 0.71 NaviQA, 0.96 CarQA); multi-sample (3-shot ensembled) prompting did not improve results enough to justify its extra cost. Human-annotation agreement (Fleiss’ κ) was substantial for the Request-orientedness (0.62) and Proactivity (0.69) judge dimensions, only moderate for Directness (0.43) — perceived verbosity/clarity is more subjective than the other dimensions. (source: Sorokin et al. 2026)

RQ1 — effectiveness

Guided search (any STELLAR variant) detects significantly more failures than random/combinatorial baselines in the majority of the 18 configurations. Non-diversified STELLAR finds the most failures overall (e.g. SafeQA/DeepSeek-V2-16B: 412.33 avg failures vs. 230.17 for STELLAR-D, 250.68 for STELLAR-DS, 198.67 for ASTRAL, 193.67 T-wise, 198.00 Random). STELLAR-D and STELLAR-DS perform comparably to each other across configurations. In CarQA-II, guided approaches don’t significantly beat non-guided baselines for most model configurations — the one case study where the effect is weaker.

RQ2 — diversity

STELLAR-DS (greedy repopulation) achieves the highest coverage in 4 of 5 systems, with SafeQA the exception (STELLAR-D performs best there, 60% vs. STELLAR-DS’s 58%); the largest single-system coverage gain over plain STELLAR is +11 percentage points on NaviQA-I (75%→86%). Overall, STELLAR-DS reaches 78% average coverage (matching random sampling’s 78%) and 89% average entropy (matching random sampling, exceeding STELLAR’s 85%), while STELLAR-D trails slightly behind DS on both metrics. Greedy vs. uniform repopulation shows no significant difference in raw failure-detection effectiveness, but up to a 3-percentage-point diversity (coverage/entropy) advantage for greedy sampling.

Qualitative failure taxonomy (CarQA, GPT-5, all seeds)

Failures were LLM-summarized, embedded, hierarchically clustered, and reviewed with a BMW domain expert (7 years, conversational-assistant testing), who confirmed all proposed types as realistic and merged related clusters into six, ordered by frequency:

  1. Partially ignored requests (most common) — multi-component or perturbed requests where some sub-actions silently don’t execute.
  2. On/off bias — system defaults toward switching/keeping a component off rather than correctly interpreting the requested state change.
  3. Unnecessary clarification questions — system asks for confirmation instead of executing an unambiguous request.
  4. Unrequested executions — system changes state of components the user never mentioned.
  5. Incorrect confirmation — system reports a change succeeded when it was not actually applied to the car state.
  6. Guard-railing false positives (least common) — a valid device-control request is refused because slang phrasing (e.g. “kill the fan”) is misclassified as malicious.

(source: Sorokin et al. 2026)

Relation to broader wiki

STELLAR-D’s judge-selection results (RQ0) are an independent empirical data point for LLM-as-Judge Evaluation: across three unrelated domains, a mid-tier model (GPT-4o-mini) beat larger/more expensive judges (GPT-4o, GPT-5-Chat) on the accuracy/cost trade-off, and multi-sample ensembling wasn’t worth its extra cost — consistent with that page’s point that judge selection benefits from empirical validation rather than defaulting to “the strongest available model.” The paper’s baseline comparison against ASTRAL (coverage-matrix test generation, no feedback loop) and its DeepJanus-inspired archive/repopulation design are discussed in the concept hub.
