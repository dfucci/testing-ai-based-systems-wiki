Summary: LGMT derives metamorphic relations from formally-proven first-order-logic (FOL) equivalence laws instead of informal lexical mutations, eliminating the semantic drift that undermines lexical MT, and finds LLMs violate logic-preserving transformations at MVR rates of 20.84-29.44% across six state-of-the-art models — with symbol renaming and conclusion rephrasing the most damaging.

Sources: Zhou et al. - 2026 - LGMT Logic-Grounded Metamorphic Testing for Evaluating the Reasoning Reliability of LLMs.pdf

Last updated: 2026-08-30

Motivation

Static logical-reasoning benchmarks (FOLIO, LogicBench, LogiQA) suffer the classic test oracle problem (labeling is expensive and doesn’t scale) plus data contamination risk. Prior metamorphic testing for LLMs/NLP relies on lexical perturbations (synonym substitution, paraphrasing), which the paper shows introduce semantic drift: a hypernym substitution like “car” -> “vehicle” is not strictly truth-preserving (a “four-wheeled vehicle” needn’t be a car), so when a model correctly changes its answer under this drift, naive lexical MT wrongly flags it as an inconsistency (a false positive). (source: Zhou et al. 2026)

Approach

LGMT derives its 20 metamorphic relations directly from formally verified FOL equivalence laws (De Morgan’s laws, contraposition, quantifier transformations, implication elimination, etc.), organized into four granularities: formula-level (MR-E: canonical rewrite rules that provably preserve logical equivalence), symbol-level (MR-S: renaming constants/predicates), premise-level (MR-P: reordering/duplicating/fusing premises), and conclusion-level (MR-C: e.g. double negation of the conclusion). Because each transformation is provably logic-preserving by construction, the correct model response must remain identical between source and follow-up — any divergence is a genuine reasoning inconsistency, not an artifact of an ambiguous paraphrase. Source test cases (premise-conclusion pairs) are drawn from FOLIO, LogicNLI, and ProverQA; the framework generated 76,298 candidate metamorphic groups, of which 3,091 were sampled for evaluation across six LLMs (GPT-5.2, Claude 4.5 Sonnet, Llama 3.3, DeepSeek Chat, o4-mini, DeepSeek Reasoner) under four prompting strategies. (source: Zhou et al. 2026)

Findings

  • All six evaluated models show substantial Metamorphic Oracle Violation Rates (MVR 20.84-29.44%), with no consistent correlation to overall model strength or reasoning-oriented design (DeepSeek Reasoner has the highest MVR at 29.44% despite being reasoning-optimized).
  • A manual audit of 360 sampled violations found an empirical False Report Rate of only 1.38% — LGMT’s violations are highly reliable, not test-generation artifacts.
  • Comparing static accuracy (Accstatic, 65.38-73.53% across models) against consistency-preserved accuracy (Acccons, 52.38-60.97%) yields a Hidden Defect Rate of 12.56-16.50% — a substantial fraction of answers that look correct under standard reference-based evaluation are not stable under logically-guaranteed-equivalent rephrasing.
  • Symbol-level (MR-S) and conclusion-level (MR-C) transformations are the most damaging (MVR 33.06% and 33.78% respectively), while premise-level (MR-P) transformations are least damaging (16.33%) — evidence that LLMs lean on lexical/token-level pattern matching rather than abstract logical structure, and that final-answer phrasing strongly affects apparent correctness independent of the underlying logic.
  • Few-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting partially mitigates these defects (e.g. GPT-5.2’s Acccons rises from 53.61% zero-shot to 63.25% few-shot CoT) but does not eliminate them, and Zero-shot CoT alone can increase hidden defects for some models.

(source: Zhou et al. 2026)

Relation to broader wiki

LGMT directly resolves the “known limitation: false positives” problem flagged on the Metamorphic Testing page — by grounding MRs in formally-proven FOL equivalences rather than informal lexical rules, it structurally rules out the semantic-drift false positives that limited Terragni 2026’s true-positive rate to ~62%. It is a sibling approach to Explanation-Guided Metamorphic Testing (which uses a semantic-verification gate to filter out invalid lexical mutations after the fact) — LGMT instead prevents invalid mutations from being generated in the first place, by construction.
